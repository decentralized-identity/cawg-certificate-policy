[[reasons-for-revoking-a-subscriber-certificate]]
===== Reasons for revoking a subscriber certificate

The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST revoke a <<certificate,certificate>> within 24 hours if one or more of the following occurs:

. the <<subscriber,subscriber>> requests in writing that the <<certification-authority,CA>> revoke the <<certificate,certificate>>,
. the <<subscriber,subscriber>> notifies the <<certification-authority,CA>> that the original <<certificate-request,certificate request>> was not authorized and does not retroactively grant authorization,
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> obtains evidence that the <<subscriber,subscriber>>’s <<private-key,private key>> corresponding to the <<public-key,public key>> in the <<certificate,certificate>> suffered a <<key-compromise,key compromise>>,
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> is made aware of a demonstrated or proven method that can easily compute the <<subscriber,subscriber>>’s <<private-key,private key>> based on the <<public-key,public key>> in the <<certificate,certificate>> (such as a Debian weak key, see https://wiki.debian.org/SSLkeys[https://wiki.debian.org/SSLkeys]), or
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> obtains evidence that the validation of domain authorization or mailbox control for any mailbox address in the <<certificate,certificate>> should not be relied upon.

[[reasons-for-revoking-a-subordinate-ca-certificate]]
===== Reasons for revoking a subordinate CA certificate

The <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> MUST revoke a <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> <<certificate,certificate>> within seven (7) days if one or more of the following occurs:

. the <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> requests revocation in writing;
. the <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> notifies the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> that the original <<certificate-request,certificate request>> was not authorized and does not retroactively grant authorization;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> obtains evidence that the <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>>’s <<private-key,private key>> corresponding to the <<public-key,public key>> in the <<certificate,certificate>> suffered a <<key-compromise,key compromise>> or no longer complies with the requirements of <<key-sizes>> and <<public-key-parameters-generation-and-quality-checking>>;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> obtains evidence that the <<certificate,certificate>> was misused;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> is made aware that the <<certificate,certificate>> was not issued in accordance with or that <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> has not complied with this document or the applicable <<certificate-policy,CP>> and/or <<certification-practice-statement,CPS>>;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> determines that any of the information appearing in the <<certificate,certificate>> is inaccurate or misleading;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> or <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> ceases operations for any reason and has not made arrangements for another <<certification-authority,CA>> to provide revocation support for the <<certificate,certificate>>;
. the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>>’s or <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>>’s right to issue <<certificate,certificates>> under these requirements expires or is revoked or terminated, unless the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> has made arrangements to continue maintaining the CRL/OCSP <<repository,repository>>; or
. revocation is required by the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>>’s <<certificate-policy,CP>> and/or <<certification-practice-statement,CPS>>.

[[who-can-request-revocation]]
==== Who can request revocation

The <<subscriber,subscriber>>, <<registration-authority,RA>>, or <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> can initiate revocation.
Additionally, <<subscriber,subscribers>>, <<relying-party,relying parties>>, <<application-software-supplier,application software suppliers>>, and other third parties MAY submit <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem reports>> informing the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>> of reasonable cause to revoke a <<certificate,certificate>>.

[[procedure-for-revocation-request]]
==== Procedure for revocation request

The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST provide a process for <<subscriber,subscribers>> to request revocation of their own <<certificate,certificates>>.
The process MUST be described in the <<certification-authority,CA>>’s <<certificate-policy,CP>> and/or <<certification-practice-statement,CPS>>.
The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST maintain a continuous 24x7 ability to accept and respond to revocation requests and <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem reports>>.

The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST provide clear instructions for reporting suspected <<private-key,private key>> compromise, <<certificate,certificate>> misuse, or other types of fraud, compromise, misuse, inappropriate conduct, or any other matter related to <<certificate,certificates>>.
The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST publicly disclose the instructions through a readily accessible online means and in Section 1.5.2 of their <<certification-practice-statement,CPS>>.

[[time-within-which-ca-must-process-the-revocation-request]]
==== Time within which CA must process the revocation request

Within 24 hours after receiving a <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem report>>, the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST investigate the facts and circumstances related to a <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem report>> and provide a preliminary report on its findings to both the <<subscriber,subscriber>> and the entity who filed the <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem report>>.

After reviewing the facts and circumstances, the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST work with the <<subscriber,subscriber>> and any entity reporting the <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem report>> or other revocation-related notice to establish whether or not the <<certificate,certificate>> will be revoked, and if so, a date on which the <<certification-authority,CA>> will revoke the <<certificate,certificate>>.
The period from receipt of the <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem report>> or revocation-related notice to published revocation MUST NOT exceed the time frame set forth in <<reasons-for-revoking-a-subscriber-certificate>>.
The date selected by the <<certification-authority,CA>> SHOULD consider the following criteria:

. the nature of the alleged problem (scope, context, severity, magnitude, risk of harm),
. the consequences of revocation (direct and collateral impacts to <<subscriber,subscribers>> and <<relying-party,relying parties>>),
. the number of <<certificate-problem-report,certificate problem reports>> received about a particular <<certificate,certificate>> or <<subscriber,subscriber>>,
. the entity making the complaint (for example, a complaint from a law enforcement official should be addressed with higher priority), and
. relevant legislation.

[[revocation-checking-requirement-for-relying-parties]]
==== Revocation checking requirement for relying parties

No stipulation.

NOTE: Following <<certificate,certificate>> issuance, a <<certificate,certificate>> may be revoked for reasons stated in <<certificate-revocation-and-suspension>>.
Therefore, <<relying-party,relying parties>> SHOULD check the revocation status of all <<certificate,certificates>> that contain a CDP or <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> pointer.

[[crl-issuance-frequency]]
==== CRL issuance frequency

For the status of <<subscriber,subscriber>> <<certificate,certificates>>: the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST update and reissue <<certificate-revocation-list,CRLs>> at least once every seven days, and the value of the `nextUpdate` field MUST NOT be more than ten days beyond the value of the `thisUpdate` field.

For the status of <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> <<certificate,certificates>>: the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST update and reissue <<certificate-revocation-list,CRLs>> at least:

. once every twelve months; and
. within 24 hours after revoking a <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> <<certificate,certificate>>.

The value of the `nextUpdate` field MUST NOT be more than twelve months beyond the value of the `thisUpdate` field.

[[on-line-revocation-status-checking-availability]]
==== On-line revocation/status checking availability

When provided, <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses MUST conform to https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6960[RFC 6960] and/or https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5019[RFC 5019].
<<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses MUST either:

. be signed by the <<certification-authority,CA>> that issued the <<certificate,certificates>> whose revocation status is being checked, or
. be signed by an <<ocsp-responder,OCSP responder>> whose <<certificate,certificate>> is signed by the <<certification-authority,CA>> that issued the <<certificate,certificate>> whose revocation status is being checked.

In the latter case, the <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> signing <<certificate,certificate>> MUST contain the `ocspSigning` <<extended-key-usage,EKU>> (1.3.6.1.5.5.7.3.9) and an extension of type `id-pkix-ocsp-nocheck`, as defined by https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6960[RFC 6960].

[[on-line-revocation-checking-requirements]]
==== On-line revocation checking requirements

<<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responders operated by the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST support the HTTP GET method, as described in https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc6960[RFC 6960] and/or https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5019[RFC 5019].

The validity interval of an <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> response is the difference in time between the `thisUpdate` and `nextUpdate` field, inclusive.
For purposes of computing differences, a difference of 3,600 seconds MUST be equal to one hour, and a difference of 86,400 seconds MUST be equal to one day, ignoring leap seconds.

For the status of <<subscriber,subscriber>> <<certificate,certificates>>:

. <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses MUST have a validity interval greater than or equal to eight hours;
. <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses MUST have a validity interval less than or equal to ten days;
. for <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses with validity intervals less than sixteen hours, then the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST update the information provided via an <<online-certificate-status-protocol,online certificate status protocol>> prior to one-half of the validity period before the `nextUpdate`; and
. for <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responses with validity intervals greater than or equal to sixteen hours, then the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST update the information provided via an <<online-certificate-status-protocol,online certificate status protocol>> at least eight hours prior to the `nextUpdate`, and no later than four days after the `thisUpdate`.

For the status of <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> <<certificate,certificates>>, the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST update information provided via <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>>:

. at least every twelve months, and
. within 24 hours after revoking a <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CA>> <<certificate,certificate>>.

If the <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responder receives a request for the status of a <<certificate,certificate>> serial number that is “unused,” then the responder SHOULD NOT respond with a “good” status.
If the <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> responder is for a <<certification-authority,CA>> that is not <<technically-constrained-subordinate-ca-certificate,technically-constrained>> in line with <<name-constraints>>, the responder MUST NOT respond with a “good” status for such requests.

A <<certificate,certificate>> serial number within an <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> request is “assigned” if a <<certificate,certificate>> with that serial number has been issued by the <<issuing-ca,issuing CA>>, using any current or previous key associated with that <<certification-authority,CA>> subject, or “unused” if otherwise.
