=== Key pair generation and installation

[[key-pair-generation-and-installation-by-cas]]
==== Key pair generation and installation by CAs

. The <<certification-authority,certification authority>> MUST maintain effective controls to provide reasonable assurance that the integrity of keys and certificates it manages is established and protected throughout their life cycles.

. The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST maintain controls to provide reasonable assurance that <<certification-authority,CA>> key pairs are generated in accordance with the <<certification-authority,CA>>’s disclosed business practices and defined procedures specified within detailed key generation ceremony scripts.

. The <<certification-authority,CA>>’s disclosed business practices MUST include but are not limited to:
.. generation of <<certification-authority,CA>> keys are undertaken in a physically- or cloud-secured environment;
.. generation of <<certification-authority,CA>> keys are performed by personnel in <<trusted-role,trusted roles>> under the principles of multiple-person control and split knowledge;
.. generation of <<certification-authority,CA>> keys occur within FIPS 140-2 Level 2 or higher, FIPS 140-3 Level 2 or higher, or an appropriate Common Criteria Protection Profile or Security Target, EAL 4 or higher cryptographic modules meeting the applicable technical and business requirements as disclosed in the <<certification-authority,CA>>’s business practices;
+

.. generation of <<certification-authority,CA>> keys are witnessed by an independent party and/or recorded; and
.. <<certification-authority,CA>> key generation activities are logged.

. The <<certification-authority,CA>> key generation script includes the following:
.. definition of roles and participant responsibilities;
.. approval for conduct of the key generation ceremony;
.. cryptographic hardware (or cloud-based <<hardware-security-module,HSM>>) and activation materials required for the ceremony;
.. specific steps performed during the key generation ceremony;
.. physical security requirements for the ceremony location (if not using cloud-based <<hardware-security-module,HSM>>);
.. procedures for secure storage of cryptographic hardware and activation materials following the key generation ceremony (if not using cloud-based <<hardware-security-module,HSM>>)
.. sign-off from participants and witnesses indicating whether key generation ceremony was performed in accordance with the detailed key generation ceremony script; and
.. notation of any deviations from the key generation ceremony script.

The <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST reject a <<certificate-request,certificate request>> if one or more of the following conditions are met:

. the <<key-pair,key pair>> does not meet the requirements set forth in <<key-sizes>> and/or <<public-key-parameters-generation-and-quality-checking>>;
. there is clear evidence that the specific method used to generate the <<private-key,private key>> was flawed;
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> is aware of a demonstrated or proven method that exposes the <<applicant,applicant>>’s <<private-key,private key>> to compromise;
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> has previously been made aware that the <<applicant,applicant>>’s <<private-key,private key>> has suffered a <<key-compromise,key compromise>>, such as through the provisions of <<reasons-for-revoking-a-subscriber-certificate>>; or
. the <<certification-authority,CA>> is aware of a demonstrated or proven method to easily compute the <<applicant,applicant>>’s <<private-key,private key>> based on the <<public-key,public key>> (such as a Debian weak key, see https://wiki.debian.org/SSLkeys[https://wiki.debian.org/SSLkeys]).

The <<certification-authority,CA>> or a <<delegated-third-party,delegated third party>> MAY generate the <<private-key,private key>> on behalf of the <<subscriber,subscriber>>.

[[private-key-delivery-to-subscriber]]
==== Private key delivery to subscriber

Parties other than the <<subscriber,subscriber>> MUST NOT archive the <<subscriber,subscriber>> <<private-key,private key>> without authorization by the <<subscriber,subscriber>>.

If the <<certification-authority,CA>> or any of its designated <<registration-authority,RAs>> become aware that a <<subscriber,subscriber>>’s <<private-key,private key>> has been communicated to a person or organization not authorized by the <<subscriber,subscriber>>, then the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST revoke all <<certificate,certificates>> that include the <<public-key,public key>> corresponding to the communicated <<private-key,private key>>.

If the <<certification-authority,CA>> or a <<delegated-third-party,delegated third party>> generates the <<private-key,private key>> on behalf of the <<subscriber,subscriber>> where the <<private-key,private keys>> will be transported to the <<subscriber,subscriber>>, then the entity generating the <<private-key,private key>> MUST either:

. transport the <<private-key,private key>> in hardware with an activation method that is equivalent to 128 bits of encryption, where the material used to activate/protect the <<private-key,private key>> (e.g., a password used to secure a PKCS-12 file) must be delivered to the <<subscriber,subscriber>> securely and separately from the container holding the <<private-key,private key>>; or
. encrypt the <<private-key,private key>> with at least 112 bits of encryption strength.

Illustrative examples include:

* Using a 128-bit AES key to wrap the <<private-key,private key>>.
* Storing the key in a PKCS-12 file encrypted using a password and algorithm whose combination provides at least 112 bits of encryption strength.
* Transporting the <<private-key,private key>> and/or PKCS-12 file and/or activation material via a TLS or other authenticated and encrypted network connection with at least 112 bits of encryption strength.
* Other approaches that meet the requirements are allowed.

The <<certification-authority,CA>> or <<delegated-third-party,delegated third party>> MUST NOT store <<subscriber,subscriber>> <<private-key,private keys>> in clear text.

[[public-key-parameters-generation-and-quality-checking]]
==== Public key parameters generation and quality checking

For RSA key pairs: the <<certification-authority,CA>> MUST confirm that the value of the public exponent is an odd number equal to 3 or more.
Additionally, the public exponent SHOULD be in the range between 2^16 + 1 and 2^256 - 1.
The modulus SHOULD also have the following characteristics: an odd number, not the power of a prime, and have no factors smaller than 752.
(See NIST SP 800-89, Section 5.3.3.)

For ECDSA key pairs: the <<certification-authority,CA>> SHOULD confirm the validity of all keys using either the ECC Full <<public-key,public key>> Validation Routine or the ECC Partial <<public-key,public key>> Validation Routine.
(See NIST SP 800-56A: Revision 2, Sections 5.6.2.3.2 and 5.6.2.3.3.)

[[key-usage-purposes-as-per-x-509-v3-key-usage-field]]
==== Key usage purposes (as per X.509 v3 key usage field)

<<private-key,Private keys>> corresponding to <<root-certification-authority-certificate,root CA certificates>> MUST NOT be used to sign <<certificate,certificates>> except in the following cases:

. Self-signed <<certificate,certificates>> to represent the <<root-certification-authority,root CA>> itself;
. <<certificate,Certificates>> for <<subordinate-ca,subordinate CAs>> and <<cross-certificate,cross certificates>>;
. <<certificate,Certificates>> for infrastructure purposes (administrative role certificates, internal <<certification-authority,CA>> operational device certificates); and
. <<certificate,Certificates>> for <<online-certificate-status-protocol,OCSP>> response verification.
